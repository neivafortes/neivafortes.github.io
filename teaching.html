Teaching

My teaching emphasizes active participation, real-world applications, and an inclusive classroom in which students feel comfortable asking questions and working through challenging material.

Teaching Philosophy

I view education as a tool that prepares students to understand the past, engage with the present, and make informed decisions about the future. My goal is to create an environment in which participation is encouraged, effort is valued, and students receive the support they need to learn.

In the classroom, I combine discussion, hands-on exercises, collaborative problem solving, and board work. I use real-world examples to make economic theory more accessible, pause regularly to check understanding, and invite students to explain concepts in their own words.

Instructor of Record

ECN 101: Introductory Microeconomics

Syracuse University · Summers 2024, 2025, and 2026

Enrollment: 12, 22, and 6 students

Course content

This intensive introductory course develops the core tools of microeconomic analysis. Topics include scarcity and choice, supply and demand, elasticity, consumer and producer behavior, market structures, externalities, and government policy. Lectures are paired with class exercises that give students repeated opportunities to apply each concept.
